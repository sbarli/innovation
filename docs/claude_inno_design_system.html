Inno · A digital adaptation of Innovation · v0.3

A civilization,
played in cards.

A design system for the digital adaptation of Innovation. Cards are landscape with the four-icon topology from the physical game so splay mechanics map cleanly. Tablet-first, light and dark, warm tabletop linens with archival warmth.

§ 01 — Type & voice

A literary serif for play.
A neutral sans for everything else.

Newsreader for display, Spectral for body — cards and tableau feel like printed artifacts. A clean sans takes over for lobbies, settings, and form chrome.

§ 02 — Color

Five colors of civilization.
Ten ages of history.

The original five card colors stay recognizable but sit slightly under, like aged ink on parchment. Each pairs with a soft tint for dogma backgrounds and tints the empty-pile state.

§ 03 — Iconography

Six dogma marks,
contained in a hex.

Castle, crown, leaf, lamp, factory, clock — the six icons that drive sharing, demanding, and defending. Filled silhouettes inside a solid hex container; legible from 14 to 72 px.

§ 04 — Card · anatomy

Landscape.
Four icons. One illustration.

Cards are horizontal, matching the physical game. The four icon positions — TL · BL · BC · BR — are the ones splaying reveals: 1 icon when splayed left, 2 when right, 3 when up. One position holds the illustration (varies by color).

§ 05 — Card · examples

Four innovations,
from four colors, four ages.

Writing (purple), Oars (red, with a demand block), Perspective (yellow), and Atomic Theory (blue). The color stripe identifies the stack; soft fills tint each dogma row.

§ 05b — Card · back

Ten ages,
ten backs.

Supply piles and the score pile show the card's back. Each back carries the age numeral, the age name, and — for ages 1–9 — the score threshold needed to claim that age's achievement (5 × age). Age 10 has no standard achievement.

§ 06 — Splay states

How splaying reveals icons.
One stack, four configurations.

The same three-card blue stack in each splay state. The strip width of each peek-out matches one icon-cell, so the resource count stays unambiguous to the player.

§ 07 — Tableau

Your board.
Five lanes, one tabletop.

Empty piles take their color's hue so the lane reads at a glance — no separate label. Populated piles let the cards speak for themselves. Open problem: opposite splays between neighboring lanes need a UX (focus mode on click is the leading proposal).

§ 08 — Gameplay chrome

Hand, actions, score.
The fixed elements of every turn.

Action bar with the four actions and two pips for actions remaining. The hand fans below. Score pile and achievements share a compact "pile" chrome; clicking the score pile expands it to show the exact card distribution.

§ 09 — Demand modal & toasts

Attacks, shares, alerts.
What the server pushes.

Demand modals interrupt and demand a decision. Toasts are passive and stack in the corner. Color coding: red for demands, blue for shares, yellow for achievements, accent for your turn.

§ 10 — Motion

How cards behave.
Meld, splay, draw, demand.

Four canonical motions, looped so you can read their character. Easing favors confident arrivals with a touch of overshoot; demands shake briefly under a red veil.
